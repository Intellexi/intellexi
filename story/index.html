The story

A company named
“I have understood.”

In Latin, intellexi is the perfect tense of intellegere — to understand. A multimedia studio chose that name in 2001, and gave itself the tagline “Be Understood.” Twenty-five years later, in the era of machines that finally can, the name reads less like a brand and more like a plan.

Act I · 2001

Be understood.

Intellexi was born in the Philippines in the first era of the web — when connections screeched through phone lines, screens were 800×600, and “multimedia” was a frontier. It was a multimedia and communications studio with a conviction that sounds obvious now and was radical then: technology should carry meaning, not just data.

The studio mastered the media of its moment. CD-ROMs — “650 megabytes of information” packed with graphics, sound, and animation. Websites that moved when the rest of the web stood still. Image campaigns, internal training, marketing that made companies feel understood by their own tools.

And people trusted it with their message: Unilever. Union Bank. De La Salle University. Radio Mindanao Network. St. Luke’s Medical School. Corporate, educational, broadcast — “one in their common motivation for competitiveness and excellence,” as the old clientele page put it.

Interlude

The quiet years.

Like many studios of that era, Intellexi’s moment passed before its idea did. Flash faded. The founder’s attention moved to new ventures — companies built, lessons compounded, and eventually Rockstar Consulting, helping businesses transform with AI.

The site went quiet. The name didn’t. Because somewhere in those two decades, the industry slowly caught up to the thing Intellexi had named itself after: it built machines that understand — language, documents, intent, businesses.

A company called “I have understood,” whose tagline was “Be Understood,” turned out to be twenty years early.

Act II · 2026

I have understood.

So Intellexi returns — not as nostalgia, but as the company it was always pointed at. The second act is an AI automation and custom software studio: the technology and software arm of Rockstar Consulting, turning ideas into intelligent systems, internal tools, customer platforms, and products.

The continuity is real, not sentimental. In 2001, the craft was making technology understandable to people. In 2026, it’s making technology that understands people — their work, their documents, their decisions — and then quietly takes the mechanical parts off their hands.

Even this site keeps the thread. The four colors that marked the old sections — blue, gold, green, coral — still mark ours. The “quoted epigraphs” still open our pages. The curly braces that once decorated the copyright line turned out to be the punctuation of our new trade. And the promise on the door is the same two words it always was.

Be understood.

The record

Twenty-five years in five moments.

  1. 2001

    The first act opens

    Intellexi launches as a multimedia and communications studio — CD-ROM publishing, web development, animation. Tagline: “Be Understood.” Minimum system requirements: a 300MHz processor and the Macromedia Flash 5 plugin.

  2. 2001–2002

    The clientele years

    Work ships for Unilever, Union Bank, De La Salle University, Radio Mindanao Network, St. Luke’s Medical School, First Asian Technologies, and Elizabeth Seton School.

  3. 2003–2004

    The archive remembers

    The Internet Archive quietly preserves snapshots of intellexi.com — the Flash splash screen, the section banners, the promise. The record that made this revival possible.

  4. The interim

    Lessons compound

    The founder builds new ventures and, with Rockstar Consulting, helps businesses transform with AI. Meanwhile, the industry builds machines that understand.

  5. 2026

    The second act

    Intellexi is revived as an AI automation and custom software studio — the technology arm of Rockstar Consulting. Same name. Same promise. New medium.
